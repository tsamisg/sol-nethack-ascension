NetHack · 18–19 October 2026

An LLM agent ascends in NetHack on its first try

by Georgios Tsamis

A language-model agent driven by GPT-6.1 Sol played a full game of NetHack and won it: it offered the Amulet of Yendor on the Astral Plane and ascended to demigoddess-hood. No human played any move. Further down is the whole game: the screen after every key press, and the key that was pressed.

guest@nethack:~/sol-ascension$ cat PRIOR_WORK

The first recorded ascension by an LLM agent was made by GPT 6 Astra on 21 September 2026: “An LLM Beat NetHack”. The work shown here was developed in parallel with it and independently of it.

What is different in this run: it was the model’s first game with the harness, and the harness was built for the thesis rather than written by the agent. There was no human guidance during the game, and the model had no access to NetHack’s source code: its only references were the NetHack wiki and spoilers.

guest@nethack:~/sol-ascension$ cat about.txt

This game is part of my thesis for the MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning at the University of West Attica, supervised by Panagiotis Kasnesis. The thesis studies language-model agents for very long tasks, with NetHack as the test bed: a won game spans tens of thousands of turns, mistakes are permanent, and success depends on knowledge that is rarely needed but decisive when it is.

Its parts: harness design for long-horizon play; learning across games through a memory the agent writes itself after reviewing each game; the knowing-doing gap, where agents know the right move (and even write it down) but don’t make it when it matters; and behaviour shaping, a self-improvement loop in which a model reviews its own games, rewrites its weak decisions and is fine-tuned on them, to make it play more carefully.

The model played through a custom harness built for the thesis. The harness lets a language model play NetHack: the model reads the game as text, decides what to do, and the harness carries its decisions out as key presses. The game ran in the NetHack Learning Environment (NLE 1.3.0), which runs NetHack 3.6.7. This was the model’s first game with the harness. It played from the first turn to the ascension, over about 17 hours, with no human moves.

All details of the work (the harness, the methods and the results) will be published with the thesis once it is finished.

guest@nethack:~/sol-ascension$ cat why-it-is-hard.txt

A won game is far longer than any model’s memory. This one took 9,057 model replies over 38,427 game turns; the model never sees more than a recent slice of it, so whatever matters later (which items are cursed, what lies on which level, when it last prayed, what it still needs for the endgame) has to survive hours of play outside its context.

Consequences arrive late: a careless choice can kill thousands of turns afterwards, and there is no undo. The game shows only text: items are unidentified, monsters can be invisible, and the screen hides most of the state. Much of the knowledge that decides a game (how to survive Medusa, what the Invocation needs, how the Riders behave) is needed once or twice, never practised. And every step goes through the game’s own interface of menus, prompts and “--More--” messages, where a single wrong key can waste or end the game.
